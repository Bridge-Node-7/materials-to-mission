# Engineered Material / Material-System Contract

## Purpose

The Materials-to-Mission Atlas has two deliberately separate public layers:

1. **Official Critical-Mineral Field** — exact USGS 2025 list membership.
2. **Engineered Materials & Systems** — real compounds, substrates, magnets, semiconductors, and material systems connected to their critical-mineral dependencies.

An engineered material is never labeled a USGS critical mineral unless an authoritative source independently designates it as such.

## Current public registry

- YIG — Yttrium Iron Garnet — primary material-system example
- GGG — Gadolinium Gallium Garnet
- YSGAG — Yttrium Scandium Gallium Aluminum Garnet
- GaN — Gallium Nitride
- GaAs — Gallium Arsenide
- GaP — Gallium Phosphide
- NdFeB — Neodymium-Iron-Boron Magnet
- SmCo — Samarium-Cobalt Magnet
- SiC — Silicon Carbide
- Synthetic Graphite

The registry is source-controlled and intentionally extensible. Future systems may be admitted when their public identity, critical-mineral relationships, use context, and source basis are reviewed.

## YIG rule

YIG is a discreet primary example because it demonstrates the full Materials-to-Mission problem:

critical inputs → qualified material stack → film/crystal growth → fabrication → research/device evidence → independent validation → acquisition/transition → deployment/sustainment.

The public pathway exposes where evidence stops. Public peer-reviewed demonstrations remain context if an earlier qualified-material or manufacturing condition is unresolved.

## Public boundary

Public inclusion of YIG/GGG does not authorize publication of:

- private supplier identities or terms;
- nonpublic laboratory data;
- protected process parameters;
- patent-sensitive implementation detail;
- private device architecture;
- customer/program-specific requirements;
- restricted, classified, export-controlled, or CUI information.

## Human authority

No engineered-material state may imply qualification, certification, acquisition approval, mission readiness, investment merit, or an automated consequential decision.
